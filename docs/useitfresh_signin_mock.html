Use It Fresh sign-in

Rebuilt so the fridge stays visible — the opaque cards are gone. Fields are longer and taller, and Google and Apple are now small icons side by side, which is what buys the room. Drawn at true 390 × 844.

What I changed

Fields are longer and wider

Form went from 57% to 76% of the screen, so the boxes run nearly the full width of the fridge’s pale panel. Field height went 38 → 46 px.

Social logins are an icon row

Two full-width provider buttons would have cost ~120 px of height. As a single row of icons it costs 46. That row is what pays for the taller fields — the two changes are linked.

Only fields and buttons are solid

Everything else — heading, slogan, labels, divider — sits straight on the artwork. Inputs need a solid fill or they don’t read as enterable, but they’re small enough that the fridge still dominates.

46 px, not 38

The original mockup’s 38 px controls are under both Apple’s 44 pt and Material’s 48 dp minimums — genuinely fiddly to tap. 46 is the compromise that keeps the airy look without being a usability problem.

Two things to decide

1 or 2?

1 is your brief exactly. 2 adds a 38% white wash behind the form only. On a good phone in normal light 1 is lovely; in direct sun on a cheap LCD, dark text on photography is where readability goes first. Your call — I’d ship 1 and keep 2 in my back pocket.

Apple sign-in is now on the critical path

Drawing the Apple icon commits you to it — and Apple requires Sign in with Apple in any iOS app offering third-party login, or review rejects it. Good news: both Google and Apple already work on Spot a Paw’s Supabase auth, so it’s a copy job. But it moves C2/C3 out of Phase 7 and into the sign-in build.
